Research architecture

Six layers separate external conditions, crypto-native drivers, capital transmission, observed outcomes, and model inference. L6 never feeds back into driver identification.

L1

Macro and global finance

Central-bank liquidity, policy and real rates, the dollar, yield curves, credit spreads, volatility, and financial stress.

L2

Bitcoin endogenous supply

Halvings, issuance, miner economics, long- and short-term holder supply, and exchange-available supply.

L3

Crypto demand drivers

Retail and institutional demand, ETF appetite, allocation willingness, stablecoin usage, and participant structure.

L4

Capital transmission

How intention becomes flow: ETF and ETP net flows, stablecoin issuance, exchange flows, and cross-market channels.

L5

Market observation

Prices, volume, volatility, drawdowns, breadth, dominance, rotation, funding, open interest, and liquidations.

L6

Cycle state output

Reserved for model inference: phase, maturity, transition risk, evidence strength, and data-quality status. Not built in Phase 1.

Governance rules

  • No look-ahead. A historical decision may only use data whose availability time was already known.
  • Append-only history. Raw data is never overwritten; corrections become new vintages.
  • Purpose isolation. L5 training, identification, and validation partitions are registered up front, with embargo checks.
  • L6 isolation. Cycle-state labels never feed back into the drivers used to explain them.
  • Evidence over narrative. Findings may be supported, unsupported, or inconclusive; independent sample size is stated.

What CMCI is not

CMCI is an environment and risk-awareness system, not a trading strategy. It does not issue buy, sell, entry, or exit instructions, and no single CMCI label should be used as the sole reason for a trade.
